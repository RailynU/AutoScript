## Boxed warning

FDA required boxed warnings, its strongest warning, on the labels of prescription opioid analgesics, opioid-containing cough products and benzodiazepines about the serious risks of using these medicines together: extreme sleepiness, respiratory depression, coma and death. The agency's review found that the number of patients prescribed both drug classes rose substantially between 2002 and 2014, as did emergency visits and overdose deaths involving both.

## Recommendations to health care professionals

Recommendations to health care professionals: limit prescribing of opioid pain medicines with benzodiazepines or other CNS depressants to patients for whom alternative treatment options are inadequate; if combined use is necessary, limit dosages and durations to the minimum required; counsel patients and caregivers about the risks of slowed or difficult breathing and sedation. A separate FDA advisory encourages naloxone availability for patients at increased risk of opioid overdose, including those taking benzodiazepines concurrently.

## Relevance to pharmacist review

Relevance to pharmacist review: a new opioid prescription for a patient with an active benzodiazepine on the profile (or the reverse) matches this boxed-warning combination; confirm necessity, duration and counseling.
