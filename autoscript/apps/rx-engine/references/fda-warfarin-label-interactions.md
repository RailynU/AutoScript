## Section 5.1 Hemorrhage

Section 5.1 (Hemorrhage): warfarin can cause major or fatal bleeding. Bleeding is more likely during the starting period and with a higher dose (resulting in a higher INR). Risk factors for bleeding include high intensity of anticoagulation (INR above 4.0), age 65 or older, history of highly variable INRs, history of gastrointestinal bleeding, hypertension, cerebrovascular disease, anemia, malignancy, trauma, renal impairment, certain genetic variants, and concomitant drugs. The label advises regular INR monitoring and tells patients to report signs of bleeding.

## Section 7 Drug interactions

Section 7 (Drug interactions): drugs that increase bleeding risk when combined with warfarin include anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and serotonin reuptake inhibitors. The label advises more frequent INR monitoring when any interacting drug is started, stopped, or changed in dose.

## CYP450 interactions

CYP450 interactions: inhibitors of CYP2C9, CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 can increase warfarin exposure and INR. Examples named in the label's interaction tables include fluconazole, sulfamethoxazole (as in sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim), metronidazole and amiodarone, among others. Inducers such as rifampin and carbamazepine can decrease INR.

## Relevance to pharmacist review

Relevance to pharmacist review: a new NSAID, antiplatelet, SSRI, or CYP2C9-inhibiting antibiotic or antifungal in a warfarin-treated patient warrants confirming prescriber awareness, considering alternatives, and arranging INR follow-up.
