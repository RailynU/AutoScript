## Scope

Scope: the Beers Criteria apply to adults aged 65 and older; they are intended to support, not replace, clinical judgment.

## Non-COX-selective NSAIDs: avoid chronic use

Non-COX-selective oral NSAIDs (including ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, meloxicam, ketorolac), first clause of the Table 2 criterion: avoid chronic use unless other alternatives are not effective and the patient can take a gastroprotective agent (proton-pump inhibitor or misoprostol). Rationale: increased risk of GI bleeding and peptic ulcer disease in high-risk groups, including those over 75 or taking oral or parenteral corticosteroids, anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents; upper GI ulcers, gross bleeding or perforation occur in a meaningful share of patients treated for 3 to 6 months and more with a year of treatment. NSAIDs can also raise blood pressure, induce kidney injury and worsen heart failure. Recommendation: avoid; evidence quality moderate; strength strong. This clause is about chronic (long-term) use; the same criterion has a second clause about short-term scheduled use, in the next section.

## Non-COX-selective NSAIDs: avoid short-term scheduled use with anticoagulants, antiplatelets or corticosteroids

Second clause of the same Table 2 criterion, new in the 2023 update: avoid short-term scheduled use of a non-COX-selective oral NSAID in combination with oral or parenteral corticosteroids, anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents, unless other alternatives are not effective and the patient can take a gastroprotective agent. Recommendation: avoid; evidence quality moderate; strength strong. Table 8 (criteria removed since 2019) records that the former drug-drug interaction row for warfarin plus NSAIDs was incorporated into this oral NSAID criterion. The clause says "scheduled": as-needed (PRN) dosing is not scheduled use in the letter of the criterion, and whether a PRN course is in practice taken on a schedule is a judgment for the pharmacist and prescriber.

## Disease-drug interactions

Disease-drug interactions (Table 3): avoid NSAIDs in patients with heart failure (fluid retention), in chronic kidney disease stage 4 or higher (creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min), and in those with a history of gastric or duodenal ulcers unless other alternatives are not effective and gastroprotection is used.

## Drug-drug interactions table

Drug-drug interactions table (Table 5), entries relevant to this collection: opioids plus benzodiazepines - avoid (increased risk of overdose and adverse events). Concurrent use of three or more CNS-active drugs - avoid (falls, fractures). Warfarin with amiodarone, ciprofloxacin, macrolide antibiotics, sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim or SSRIs - avoid when possible; if used together, monitor the INR closely (increased bleeding risk). Warfarin plus NSAIDs is no longer a row of this table in the 2023 update: it is covered by the oral NSAID criterion above. The table has no ACE inhibitor plus NSAID entry; that interaction is a labeling statement (see the lisinopril and ibuprofen labels in this collection).

## Relevance to pharmacist review

Relevance to pharmacist review: for a patient 65 or older, an NSAID prescription matches the Beers NSAID criterion when the use is chronic, or when the use is short-term and scheduled and the patient takes an anticoagulant, an antiplatelet agent or a corticosteroid; an NSAID for a patient with CKD, heart failure or ulcer history matches a disease-drug entry. Each match should prompt a conversation with the prescriber. The criteria do not grade an individual prescription; they flag a pattern that needs a decision.
