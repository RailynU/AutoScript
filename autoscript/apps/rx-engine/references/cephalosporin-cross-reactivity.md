## Overall cross-reactivity rate

Historical estimates of about 10% cross-reactivity between penicillins and cephalosporins are now considered inflated, partly due to early contamination of cephalosporin products with penicillin. Contemporary estimates place overall cross-reactivity at roughly 2% or less for patients with a confirmed penicillin allergy.

## Side-chain similarity

Cross-reactivity is driven mainly by shared R1 side chains rather than the shared beta-lactam ring. Pairs with identical or similar side chains carry higher risk: amoxicillin with cefadroxil, cefprozil and cefatrizine; ampicillin with cephalexin, cefaclor and cephradine. Cephalosporins with dissimilar side chains (for example cefuroxime, ceftriaxone, cefdinir relative to amoxicillin) carry low cross-reactivity.

## 2022 practice parameter guidance

The 2022 practice parameter supports administering cephalosporins with dissimilar side chains to patients with a history of penicillin allergy without prior testing, while advising caution and specialist evaluation for patients with a history of anaphylaxis or severe cutaneous adverse reactions.

## Relevance to pharmacist review

Relevance to pharmacist review: a cephalosporin prescribed to a penicillin-allergic patient is usually a judgment call informed by the specific agents involved and the reaction history, which is why the system flags it for assessment rather than treating it as a contraindication.
